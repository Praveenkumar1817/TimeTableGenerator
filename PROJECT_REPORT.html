ABSTRACT

Academic scheduling in higher educational institutions is a notoriously NP-hard combinatorial optimization problem characterized by intricate interdependent constraints spanning student cohorts, faculty availabilities, physical laboratory infrastructures, and departmental guidelines. Manual timetable compilation routinely demands dozens of faculty hours each semester, yet invariably results in accidental instructor double-booking, sub-optimal room utilization, isolated single-hour lab allocations, and irregular weekly workload distributions across student cohorts.

This project presents an enterprise-grade, full-stack AI-Based Automated College Timetable Generation and Constraint Optimization System. The architecture combines an industrial-strength mathematical optimization engine powered by Google OR-Tools CP-SAT (Constraint Programming over Boolean Satisfiability) with a high-performance Python FastAPI asynchronous backend, SQLAlchemy ORM persistence, and a modern, responsive React/TypeScript Single Page Application (SPA). The application models the entire departmental ecosystem into a mathematical Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) containing thousands of boolean decision variables that represent the assignment of classes across sections, subjects, days, periods, instructors, and dedicated venues.

The optimization engine models both rigorous hard constraints (zero faculty or student section collisions, strict adherence to weekly credit hours, non-empty initial morning periods, continuous 3-hour laboratory allocations without mid-session breaks, dedicated multi-faculty lab requirements, and blocked institutional Association slots) and soft objective preferences (optimal room utilization and balanced daily distributions). On a realistic departmental benchmark comprising 6 academic sections (2nd, 3rd, and 4th years), 48 subjects, 25 faculty members, and 11 specialized physical classrooms and laboratories, the CP-SAT engine solved all 168 weekly period slots to mathematical optimality in under 1.2 seconds, achieving zero schedule collisions, 100% adherence to contiguous 3-hour practical blocks, and 100% first-period morning coverage.

The system includes an intuitive administrative web dashboard providing section-grouped curriculum management, multi-faculty pool mapping, dynamic deletion with foreign-key integrity, one-click schedule generation, an instant section expand/focus modal, and clean CSV/PDF document export capabilities.

INTRODUCTION

1.1 BACKGROUND OF THE PROJECT DOMAIN

Higher education administration involves complex logistical workflows, among which course timetabling is arguably the most challenging and critical. At technical universities and engineering institutions, timetable generation involves coordinating dozens of academic departments, hundreds of faculty members, diverse batches of students, and scarce infrastructure such as specialized computing laboratories, lecture halls, and instrumentation centers.

Mathematically, the University Course Timetabling Problem (UCTP) is categorized under Operations Research as a combinatorial optimization problem proven to be NP-complete. As the number of constraints increases linearly, the possible permutation search space expands exponentially. Traditional heuristic methods, such as greedy manual scheduling or basic genetic algorithms without exact bounding, frequently suffer from local minima traps, high computational runtimes, and subtle schedule violations that emerge only after classes have commenced.

In contemporary engineering curricula, scheduling is governed by diverse constraints. Beyond ensuring that an instructor is not assigned to two different lecture halls simultaneously, modern institutions must coordinate:

  • Credit Hour Compliance: Theory courses requiring 3 to 4 weekly periods, and practical laboratory courses requiring 3 continuous hours in a single session.
  • Laboratory Staffing Pools: High student-to-instructor ratios during practical sessions require 2 or 3 faculty members to co-teach the same lab simultaneously.
  • Dedicated Infrastructure: High-performance computing labs, AI computing servers, and network simulation setups can only be hosted in specific venues.
  • Institutional Activity Blocks: Co-curricular windows, such as Department Association hours, must remain completely free of academic lectures.
  • Ergonomic Student Workload: Avoiding idle first-morning hours, preventing cognitive fatigue by capping daily classroom hours, and ensuring equitable Friday class distributions.

This project, AI-Based Automated College Timetable Generation and Constraint Optimization System, introduces a full-stack automated platform leveraging Constraint Programming over Boolean Satisfiability (CP-SAT) via Google OR-Tools to mathematically eliminate collisions and synthesize optimal schedules in seconds.

2.2 COMPONENT DESCRIPTION

2.2.1 Client-Side Presentation Tier (frontend/)

Built as a Single Page Application (SPA) using React 18, TypeScript, Vite, and Tailwind CSS. The interface adopts a desktop administrative layout with a dark sidebar navigation drawer and responsive content viewport. Components include:

  • Full Focus Modal: An interactive overlay dialog enabling operators to view a single section's timetable in large typography with dedicated single-section printing.
  • Section-Grouped Curriculum Manager: Groups subjects and faculty allocations directly under their respective cohorts, displaying total weekly hours and lab venue allocations.
  • Document Generation Service: Client-side Blob generation for RFC 4180 compliant CSV export and CSS paged media integration (`@media print`) for clean PDF output.
11

2.2.2 Backend Application Tier (backend/app/api/)

Powered by Python FastAPI, this layer exposes high-throughput, asynchronous RESTful endpoints. The API implements CORS middleware to support client requests across origins.

  • CRUD API Router (api/crud.py): Provides standardized GET, POST, and DELETE endpoints for academic sections, subjects, faculty members, rooms, and allocations. Implements cascade deletion integrity to prevent orphaned allocations.
  • Optimization API Router (api/timetable.py): Assembles the complete departmental scheduling payload from the database and passes it to the CP-SAT solver. Upon finding a feasible or optimal solution, it cross-references integer entity IDs with human-readable names and merges institutional fixed slots (Association) into a unified schedule payload.
  • Pydantic Data Contracts (schemas/domain.py): Validates schema structure on ingress and egress. Optional typing ensures robust serialization even when attributes like subject codes or lab priorities are unassigned.

2.2.3 AI Optimization Engine Tier (backend/app/solver/)

The core mathematical engine is implemented in `timetable_solver.py` using Google OR-Tools CP-SAT. Rather than using randomized heuristic search, CP-SAT operates on Constraint Programming over Boolean Satisfiability, evaluating algebraic bounds to prune millions of infeasible combinations instantly.

  • Variable Representation: Boolean decision variables $X_{s, c, d, p} \in \{0, 1\}$ represent whether section $s$ attends course $c$ on day $d$ at period $p$. Auxiliary boolean tensors $F_{s, c, d, p, f}$ and $R_{s, c, d, p, r}$ link specific instructors and venues.
  • Continuous Laboratory Logic: Models multi-period laboratory allocations using contiguous session variables $Y_{s, c, d, p_{start}}$, guaranteeing that a 3-hour lab session executes as an unbroken block on a single day.
  • Non-Empty Morning Hour Logic: Strictly enforces that $\sum_{c} X_{s, c, d, 0} = 1$, ensuring students are greeted with active instruction at Period 1 every day.

2.2.4 Data Persistence Tier (backend/app/models/)

Database entities are mapped via SQLAlchemy ORM to a relational SQLite database (`timetable.db`). The relational design utilizes standard primary-key foreign-key relationships:

  • `AcademicYear` → `Section` (1-to-many relationship).
  • `Subject` → `SubjectAssignment` ← `Section` (many-to-many relationship with associative parameters).
  • `SubjectAssignment` stores JSON-encoded arrays for `faculty_pool` and `eligible_labs` to support multi-instructor staffing and venue priorities.
  • `FixedSlot` models reserved non-academic periods (e.g. Department Association).
12

2.2.5 Data Model Specifications

The relational entity architecture is implemented across six primary database tables:

  • sections: Stores the section identifier, section name (e.g., "Year 2 - Sec A"), and foreign key to `academic_years`.
  • subjects: Encapsulates course nomenclature, optional catalog course code, subject classification enum (`THEORY`, `LAB`, `SPECIAL_ACTIVITY`), and weekly period quota (3 to 4 hours).
  • faculty: Maintains academic titles, instructor names, and optional relational links to individual period unavailability constraints.
  • rooms: Represents physical academic venues, capacity limits, and boolean classification flags designating specialized computing or instrumentation labs.
  • subject_assignments: The central mapping entity binding a subject to a section, specifying required instructor count (1 for theory, 2-3 for practicals), candidate instructor pools, and eligible laboratory room IDs.
  • fixed_slots: Encapsulates institutional scheduling exclusions, specifying section ID, day index, period index, and activity label (e.g., "Association").

Because the entire data layer is abstracted behind SQLAlchemy ORM, the underlying database engine can be shifted from local SQLite to enterprise PostgreSQL or MySQL simply by modifying the database connection URL in `config.py` without code refactoring.

4.2.3 CP-SAT Optimization Engine (solver/timetable_solver.py)

The optimization engine translates academic constraints into an exact boolean satisfiability problem. The mathematical formulation is structured as follows:

Decision Variables:

X_{s, c, d, p} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall s \in S, c \in C_s, d \in D, p \in P

where $s$ denotes section, $c$ denotes subject, $d$ denotes day ($0 \dots 4$), and $p$ denotes period ($0 \dots 6$). Auxiliary variables include:

F_{s, c, d, p, f} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \text{(Faculty assignment)}, \quad R_{s, c, d, p, r} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \text{(Room assignment)}

Hard Constraint 1: Section Clash Elimination

A student section can attend at most one course during any period:

\sum_{c \in C_s} X_{s, c, d, p} \le 1 \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D, p \in P

Hard Constraint 2: Non-Empty Morning Hour (Period 1 Guarantee)

Every section must have a class scheduled during the first period ($p = 0$) every day:

\sum_{c \in C_s} X_{s, c, d, 0} = 1 \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D

Hard Constraint 3: Continuous 3-Hour Laboratory Blocks

For each laboratory course $c \in C_{lab}$, all $K = 3$ hours must occur as a single contiguous block on a single day. Let $Y_{s, c, d, p_{start}} \in \{0, 1\}$ represent the start of the 3-hour session:

\sum_{d \in D} \sum_{p_{start}=0}^{4} Y_{s, c, d, p_{start}} = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad X_{s, c, d, p} = \sum_{p_{start} \le p < p_{start} + 3} Y_{s, c, d, p_{start}}

Hard Constraint 4: Multi-Faculty Laboratory Staffing

If laboratory $c$ requires $M$ instructors simultaneously ($M \in \{2, 3\}$):

\sum_{f \in Pool(s, c)} F_{s, c, d, p, f} = M \cdot X_{s, c, d, p}
17

Hard Constraint 5: Faculty Clash Elimination

An instructor can teach at most one class across all sections and courses during any single period:

\sum_{s \in S} \sum_{c \in C_s : f \in Pool(s, c)} F_{s, c, d, p, f} \le 1 \quad \forall f \in F_{total}, d \in D, p \in P

Hard Constraint 6: Laboratory Venue Clash Elimination

A laboratory room can host at most one practical session during any period:

\sum_{s \in S} \sum_{c \in C_s : r \in Eligible(s, c)} R_{s, c, d, p, r} \le 1 \quad \forall r \in R_{labs}, d \in D, p \in P

Hard Constraint 7: Institutional Association Exclusion

For fixed activity slots ($d_{fixed}, p_{fixed}$) reserved for Association meetings:

X_{s, c, d_{fixed}, p_{fixed}} = 0 \quad \forall c \in C_s

Hard Constraint 8: Daily Load Balancing Across All 5 Days

Every section must have between 3 and 6 periods of classes every day, preventing empty Fridays:

3 \le \sum_{c \in C_s} \sum_{p \in P} X_{s, c, d, p} \le 6 \quad \forall s \in S, d \in D

5.5 DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS

The quantitative and visual results demonstrate the advantages of applying Constraint Programming (CP-SAT) over traditional randomized genetic algorithms or heuristic hill-climbing for academic course scheduling. By formulating curriculum rules as boolean satisfiability constraints, the solver explores the search tree using exact bounds, pruning millions of invalid branches in milliseconds. This guarantees complete absence of instructor or venue clashes and 100% adherence to institutional policies.

Identified Limitations and Future Enhancements:

  • Individual Faculty Preference Optimization: Currently, the engine handles instructor unavailability as a hard boolean constraint. Future revisions can incorporate soft objective penalties representing preferred teaching time slots (e.g. morning vs. afternoon preferences) to enhance faculty satisfaction.
  • Elective Course Splitting: The current formulation assumes standard cohort-based section enrollments. Modeling open electives, where students from multiple departments select concurrent modular courses, will require multi-commodity flow constraints.
  • Dynamic Teacher Leave Re-Allocation: Integrating an automatic substitute-faculty recommender to dynamically repair schedules when an instructor takes unexpected leave will further improve operational utility.

In conclusion, the AI-Based Automated College Timetable Generation and Constraint Optimization System delivers a production-ready, mathematically verified solution to a complex administrative challenge, reducing timetable compilation time from several days to under two seconds while maintaining complete constraint integrity.
